Half a century ago 3D quantum contextual hypergraphs were 
generated. Practically all of them were critical meaning that a
removal of any of their hyperedges would render them
noncontextual, i.e., that no subhypergraph of such trimmed 
hypegraphs would be contextual. And most of them were the
smallest samples generated by a chosen set of vector
components that serve building their vectors represented by 
their vertices. Unfortunately, at the time, authors of these 
hypergraphs dropped vertices that belonged to just one
hyperedge, apparently in an attempt to make their graphical
presentation look simpler, without noticing, or perhaps ignoring
the fact that they stopped being critical. Unfortunately, because
even half a century later, e.g., in 2025 in Physical Review Letters,
authors still do not notice that such hypergraphs are not critical.
In this video we give two examples, one from that time and a
most recent one, that illustrate the problem.  In particular, we
show that they are neither critical, nor the smallest,
nor the simplest hypergraphs of their kind.
